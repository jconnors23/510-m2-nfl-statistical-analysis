# Key Question

## What We Are Asking

Every Friday on his show *The Herd*, Colin Cowherd names five NFL teams he favors against the point spread. The segment is called the *Blazin' 5*, and it reaches a large national audience of casual sports fans. Many of those fans also place small bets. The question this project asks is direct: **when Cowherd endorses a team, does the betting market move in that team's favor more than it would have on its own?**

Betting markets set a price for each team, and that price can be read as the team's implied chance of winning. If a flood of casual bettors follows Cowherd's picks, sportsbooks may nudge those prices to balance their own risk. If that happens, the picked teams should show a larger shift in their implied chance of winning over the hours after the show than teams he did not mention.

## Why This Is Interesting

There are two competing stories, and the study is set up to tell them apart.

- **The crowd moves the price.** Millions of casual viewers act on the same five picks within the same few hours. That concentrated, one-directional betting could push the price for those teams noticeably, even if the underlying game has not changed.
- **The market absorbs it as noise.** Professional bettors and the sheer size of the overall betting pool may swamp the casual money, leaving the price essentially where it would have been. In this version, a celebrity pick is just one small voice among many.

Neither story is obviously correct ahead of time, which is what makes the question worth a real measurement rather than a guess.

## What We Expect to See

The honest expectation is a small effect at most. A single weekend gives only about five picked games and ten or so unpicked ones, which is a thin slice of data. Even if a real tout effect exists, one weekend may not show it clearly. The point of this first run is less to settle the question and more to see whether the measurement works and whether the direction of the shift is worth chasing with a bigger sample later.

For how we turn this expectation into a testable comparison, see the [Planned Experiment](./the-experiment.md).
