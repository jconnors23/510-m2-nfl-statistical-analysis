## How to Read the Outcome

When the data is collected, the study will produce three things:

- **The average shift for each group.** One number for the picked games, one for the unpicked games, each stated as a change in win probability over the day.
- **The gap between the groups.** How much larger or smaller the picked-game shift was, with a range around it showing how precise that gap is.
- **A test of whether the gap is real.** A statistical test will say whether a difference this size could easily have happened by chance in a sample this small.

If the picked games shifted clearly more than the unpicked games and the test says that gap is unlikely to be chance, that is early support for a tout effect. If the two groups look alike, or the gap is well within what chance could produce, that points toward the market absorbing the picks as noise.

## What This Study Cannot Claim

Even a clean result here is a first look, not a verdict.

- One weekend is far short of the sample a confident answer would need, as explained on the [Planned Experiment](./the-experiment.md) page. A striking result would justify a bigger study, not close the question.
- The study measures that prices moved, not *why*. It cannot by itself separate Cowherd's influence from injury news or other Friday events.
- The findings describe one sportsbook on one weekend. They do not automatically carry over to other books, other analysts, or other sports.

## What Comes Next

The natural follow-up is to repeat the same recording across many weekends until the sample reaches the size the design calls for. With a full season in hand, the same comparison would carry real weight, and the confounders noted on [Data Collection](./data-collection.md) could be handled more carefully.
